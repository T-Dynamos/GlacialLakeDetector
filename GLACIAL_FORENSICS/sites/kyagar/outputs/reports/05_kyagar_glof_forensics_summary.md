## 2. Kyagar Glacier vs Kyagar Tso Differentiation (Mandatory Scientific Verification)
- **Kyagar Glacier Lake (`HMAGLOF_KYAGAR_001`):** Located at **35.675°N, 77.185°E** in the Trans-Karakoram Shaksgam Valley (elevation 4,785 m a.s.l.). Surge-type dynamic ice-dammed lake subject to recurring catastrophic GLOFs into the Yarkant River.
- **Kyagar Tso (`KYAGAR_TSO_RUPSHU`):** Located at **33.1064°N, 78.3045°E** in the Rupshu plateau of southeastern Ladakh (elevation 4,570 m a.s.l.). High-altitude endorheic saline/brackish lake occupying a tectonic plateau depression with no outlet and **zero GLOF susceptibility**.
- **Conclusion:** The two water bodies share an anglicized toponym but are geographically, glaciologically, and hydrologically completely distinct. Kyagar Tso must never be modeled as a GLOF source.

## 3. Geopolitical and Earth Observation Accessibility Analysis
- **Geographic Access:** Kyagar Glacier is situated in the Shaksgam Valley (Trans-Karakoram Tract), an uninhabited, hyper-remote glaciated gorge with no terrestrial access from the Indian side (separated by the Great Karakoram Crest) and subject to severe political access restrictions.
- **Earth Observation Primacy:** Field-based gauging stations are absent; synthetic aperture radar (Sentinel-1 C-band RTC, NISAR L-band) provides the *sole* continuous, cloud-penetrating early-warning mechanism capable of detecting lake impoundment prior to subglacial breakout.

# GLACIAL FORENSICS — Outburst Quantification & Ice-Dam Dynamics: Kyagar Glacier (2018)

## 1. Executive Summary
Kyagar Glacier is a surge-type Karakoram glacier with a well-documented cyclic history of blocking the Shaksgam River. Analysis of the August 2018 outburst event reveals the classic signature of an ice-dammed lake failure through subglacial conduit enlargement, releasing an estimated ~4.8 to 6.8 million m³ of water into the Yarkant drainage network.

## 4. Ice Dam Failure Mechanism
1. **Surge Advancement:** The surge of Kyagar Glacier pushed an active ice tongue across the Shaksgam River bed, sealing the gorge and impounding meltwater from upstream Karakoram glaciers.
2. **Hydrostatic Flotation vs Subglacial Tunneling:** As water depth reached ~50 m at the ice face, basal hydrostatic pressure ($ho_w g h$) approached the cryostatic overburden pressure of the ice ($ho_i g H$). Subglacial water penetrated along the bed, triggering rapid viscous frictional melting and channel enlargement (Roethlisberger channel dynamics).
3. **Progressive Evacuation:** Pre-outburst lake area was **27.21 ha** on 12 August 2018. Within 6 days (by 18 August), the lake had catastrophically drained; by 24 August, residual water was **14.56 ha** (-46.5%), and by 17 September it fell to **5.79 ha** (-78.7%).
